### Introducing MahiTime: A Perfect System for Time Measurement

If you, like me, have OCD and crave precision and order in all things, you might be frustrated with our current concept of time. Random numbers, errors, and patches in our calendars can be quite annoying. So, I propose a new concept of time units to fix these imperfections—or at least, I'll die trying.

#### The Basics of MahiTime

First, we redefine our basic unit of time. Instead of one second, we use one **MahiSecond**, which is exactly **0.3945 seconds**. This allows us to create a more orderly system of time measurement:

- **1 MahiSecond** = 0.3945 seconds
- **1 MahiMinute** = 100 MahiSeconds (approximately 40 seconds)
- **1 MahiHour** = 100 MahiMinutes (approximately 66 minutes)

A day under this new system, called a **MahiDay**, will consist of exactly **20 MahiHours**. Unlike the current system, where a day is not precisely 24 hours but roughly 23 hours, 57 minutes, and 4.09 seconds, the MahiDay will be exactly consistent with Earth's axial rotation.

#### The Challenge of Synchronizing Days and Years

Now, let's tackle the more complex problem of synchronizing days, weeks, months, seasons, and years. The Earth's orbital rotation (around the Sun) and axial rotation (around itself) are not synchronized, meaning no perfect whole number of spins fits neatly into one orbit. This discrepancy is the root of many calendar issues.

Currently, we deal with these discrepancies by using leap years, since a tropical year is approximately 365.24219 days long. Our goal is to minimize these errors.

#### Using MahiDays for a Year

In our new system, a year will be **366.242 MahiDays** which is similar to the error we experience with 24-hour days and 365-day years, requiring a leap year every four years. To eliminate leap years, we need to adjust the MahiSeconds again, introducing new errors to day measurement. However, by carefully choosing MahiSeconds, we can reduce the error to something much lower than our current days, while having a perfect year. For that cause, we update the MahiSeconds to **0.431105536 seconds**. Therefore:

- **1 MahiSecond** = 0.431105536 seconds
- **1 MahiMinute** = 100 MahiSeconds (~43 seconds)
- **1 MahiHour** = 100 MahiMinutes (~71 minutes)
- **1 MahiDay** = 20 MahiHours
- **1 Year** = 366 MahiDays with no leap years

#### Adjusting the Seasons

Here’s how the seasons break down using our new unit:

- **Spring**: 92.993 MahiDays (rounded to 93)
- **Summer**: 93.794 MahiDays (rounded to 94)
- **Autumn**: 89.986 MahiDays (rounded to 90)
- **Winter**: 89.185 MahiDays (rounded to 89)

To balance these, we can consider:
- **Spring**: 93 MahiDays
- **Summer**: 93 MahiDays
- **Autumn**: 90 MahiDays
- **Winter**: 90 MahiDays

This distribution provides a more even seasonal layout and aligns well with the Persian calendar.

#### Months and Weeks in MahiTime

To maintain order and avoid leap years:
- **Spring and Summer**: 31 MahiDays each month
- **Autumn and Winter**: 30 MahiDays each month
